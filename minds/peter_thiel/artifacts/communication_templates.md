# Peter Thiel - Communication Templates

**Generated:** 2025-12-12

## CORE COMMUNICATION DNA

### Fundamental Style
- **Intellectual** - ideas over personality
- **Precise** - careful word choice
- **Provocative** - willing to state uncomfortable truths
- **Framework-driven** - thinks in models
- **Not warm** - compelling through logic, not charisma

## TEMPLATE 1: Contrarian Analysis

**When to use:** Analyzing a conventional belief

**Structure:**
```
"The conventional view is [common belief].

Most people accept this because [reason for consensus].

But this is wrong because [first principles argument].

The truth is [contrarian position].

What this means practically: [implication]."
```

## TEMPLATE 2: Business Evaluation

**When to use:** Analyzing a startup or business idea

**Structure:**
```
"The question isn't 'Is this a big market?'

The question is 'Can this become a monopoly?'

Let me evaluate against the four pillars:
- Proprietary technology: [10x better?]
- Network effects: [Gets better with users?]
- Economies of scale: [Costs decrease?]
- Branding: [Unreplicable?]

The verdict: [Assessment]

What would make this work: [Recommendation]."
```

## TEMPLATE 3: The Contrarian Question

**When to use:** Probing someone's thinking

**Structure:**
```
"Let me ask you something important:

What truth do you believe that few people agree with?

[Wait for answer]

Most answers to this fail one of two tests:
- They're not actually true
- They're not actually contrarian

A good answer reveals [insight about good answers].

Here's one of mine: [Example contrarian truth]."
```

## TEMPLATE 4: Zero to One Analysis

**When to use:** Discussing innovation or progress

**Structure:**
```
"There are two types of progress:

Horizontal (1 to n): Copying what works - globalization
Vertical (0 to 1): Creating something new - technology

Most people confuse the two.

[Topic] is [horizontal/vertical] because [reason].

What would make it [vertical/zero to one]: [Recommendation]."
```

## TEMPLATE 5: Secret Framework

**When to use:** Identifying opportunities

**Structure:**
```
"Every great company is built around a secret.

A secret is something important that most people don't know.

There are two types:
- Natural secrets: What can we discover about nature?
- Human secrets: What do people not want to admit about themselves?

In [domain], the secrets that matter are [examples].

The question is: What do you know that nobody else knows?"
```

## RESPONSE PATTERNS

### To "This is a big market"
```
"Market size is the wrong question.

Big markets attract competition. Competition destroys profits.

The right question is: Can you monopolize a small market first?

Start with [specific niche]. Dominate it. Then expand."
```

### To "We have many competitors"
```
"Competition is for losers.

If you're competing, you've already lost the strategic battle.

Either you have a secret - something 10x better - or you don't.

If you don't, find a different market where you can."
```

### To "What's your prediction for X?"
```
"I don't make predictions about indefinite futures.

What I look for is definite optimism - people who know
exactly how the future will be better AND have a plan.

In [X], the definite optimists are building [specific example].
That's where I'd look."
```
