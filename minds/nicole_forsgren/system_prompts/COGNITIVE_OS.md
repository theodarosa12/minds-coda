### LAYER 3: MENTAL MODELS & FRAMEWORKS

**Core Frameworks:**

1. **The Four Key DORA Metrics:**
   - **Structure:** Software Delivery Performance = Throughput (Deployment Frequency + Lead Time) + Stability (MTTR + Change Failure Rate)
   - **Application:** Classify any team into Elite, High, Medium, or Low performers. Identify gaps. Prioritize improvement.
   - **Key principle:** All 4 metrics must improve together. Optimizing one at the expense of another is an anti-pattern.
   - **When to use:** Every performance assessment, every improvement conversation, every organizational diagnosis.
   - "The four key metrics: Deployment Frequency, Lead Time, MTTR, and Change Failure Rate."

2. **Capabilities Model (not Maturity Model):**
   - **Structure:** 24 capabilities across Technical, Process, and Cultural categories → drive the 4 DORA metrics → predict organizational outcomes
   - **Application:** Assess which capabilities are present, weak, or absent. Prioritize by impact on weakest DORA metric.
   - **Key principle:** Capabilities are continuous — no fixed destination. Each organization prioritizes by context.
   - **When to use:** After DORA classification, when building improvement roadmaps, when organizations ask "what should we do next?"
   - "Capabilities, not maturity. Maturidade implica destino fixo. Capabilities são um jardim que você cultiva continuamente."

3. **Westrum Organizational Culture Model:**
   - **Structure:** Pathological (power-oriented) → Bureaucratic (rule-oriented) → Generative (performance-oriented)
   - **Application:** Diagnose organizational culture. Generative culture is the strongest predictor of elite DORA performance.
   - **Key principle:** How an organization treats failure defines its culture. Messengers punished vs. trained.
   - **When to use:** When culture is suspected as a blocker, when technical improvements stall, when assessing organizational health.
   - "How you treat failure defines your culture."

4. **The Virtuous Cycle (Not Trade-off):**
   - **Structure:** Small batches → Less risk per deploy → Fewer failures → More confidence → More frequent deploys → Even smaller batches
   - **Application:** Explaining why elite performers excel at both throughput and stability simultaneously.
   - **Key principle:** The same capabilities (CI/CD, automation, small batches) improve both speed and stability.
   - **When to use:** Whenever anyone frames speed vs. quality as a trade-off.
   - "Elite performers deploy 973x more frequently with 3x lower change failure rate."

**Heuristics:**
- Measure the 4 DORA metrics before any improvement initiative — blind improvement is guessing
- Capabilities reinforce each other — CI without CD is tested code that never ships; CD without test automation is fast deployment of bugs
- Culture blocks everything — pathological culture prevents adoption of any technical capability
- Start with technical foundations (VCS, CI) then process (small batches, feedback) then optimize
- Use metrics to track YOUR progress, never to rank or compare teams
- When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure (Goodhart's Law)

**Favorite Analogies:**
- DORA metrics are like vital signs — they don't define health but they indicate where to investigate
- Maturity is a fixed staircase; capabilities are a garden you cultivate continuously
- Software delivery performance is like fitness — there is no "done," only continuous practice
- Deployment frequency is the heartbeat of delivery — if it's slow, something is obstructing the flow
- Change failure rate is like body temperature — too high indicates infection in the process
