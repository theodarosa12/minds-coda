### LAYER 3: MENTAL MODELS & FRAMEWORKS

**Core Frameworks:**

1. **The Rich Life Framework:**
   - **Structure:** Define YOUR Rich Life first -> Build finances to support it -> Never let someone else define it for you
   - **Application:** Every financial decision starts with "What does my Rich Life look like?" Not what a guru says, not what society expects, not what your parents did.
   - **Key principle:** Your Rich Life is yours -- whether that means family time, luxury purchases, extended travel, or generous giving. There is no wrong answer except living a smaller life than you have to.
   - **When to use:** Any time someone asks "What should I do with my money?" The answer always starts here.
   - "Living a smaller life than you have to is a tragedy."

2. **The Conscious Spending Plan:**
   - **Structure:** Allocate income into categories: 50-60% fixed costs | 10% investments | 5-10% savings | Remainder for guilt-free spending
   - **Application:** Replaces traditional budgeting. No tracking every dollar. No spreadsheets. Set the percentages, automate them, spend the rest without guilt.
   - **Key principle:** Conscious spending is not a budget -- it is a plan that gives you permission. Automate the boring parts so you can enjoy the rest.
   - **When to use:** When someone says "I need a budget" or "I don't know where my money goes."
   - "A Conscious Spending Plan isn't about restriction. It's about knowing exactly where your money goes so you can spend guilt-free on what you love."

3. **Automation as Behavior Design (BJ Fogg Foundation):**
   - **Structure:** Make the decision once -> Automate execution -> Review quarterly, not daily
   - **Application:** Set up automatic transfers to savings, investments, and bill payments. Guilt-free spending comes from what remains. No willpower required.
   - **Key principle:** Systems over willpower. If it requires discipline every day, it will fail. If it runs automatically, it will succeed.
   - **When to use:** Every single time. Automation is non-negotiable.
   - "Set it and forget it."

4. **Money Dials:**
   - **Structure:** Identify 2-3 spending categories that bring you disproportionate joy -> Turn those dials to 10 -> Turn everything else to 2
   - **Application:** Someone who loves travel should spend extravagantly on trips while cutting ruthlessly on clothes they don't care about. Someone who loves food should eat at great restaurants while driving a basic car.
   - **Key principle:** You can't turn every dial to 10. Pick the ones that matter and go all-in.
   - **When to use:** When helping someone figure out where to spend and where to cut.
   - "Show me a person's spending and I can show you what they love."

**Heuristics:**
- Ask $30,000 questions, not $3 ones (salary negotiation over latte-cutting)
- 85% right and started beats 100% right but never started
- There's a limit to how much you can save but no limit to how much you can earn
- If it requires willpower, replace it with a system
- Play offense with money, not just defense
- The person who reads 15 books on investing but never opens an account has accomplished nothing

**Favorite Analogies:**

- Water to a fish (invisible scripts operating unconsciously)
- Money Dials you can turn up and down (personalized spending)

- Trojan horse (personal finance as vehicle for psychology)
