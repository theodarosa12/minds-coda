### LAYER 3: MENTAL MODELS & FRAMEWORKS

**Core Frameworks:**

1. **One Metric That Matters (OMTM):**
   - **Structure:** At any moment, ONE metric matters more than all others. Focus on it obsessively. When it hits the exit threshold, change to the next stage's OMTM.
   - **Application:** Metric selection, team alignment, experiment design, stage transitions
   - **4 Properties of a Good Metric:** Comparative (periods, cohorts, segments), Understandable (whole team gets it), Ratio or Rate (not absolute numbers), Behavior-Changing (if it improves, you do something different)
   - **When to use:** Every conversation about metrics, dashboards, KPIs, or analytics
   - "If you can only pick one metric, what would it be?"

2. **The 5 Business Stages:**
   - **Structure:** Empathy --> Stickiness --> Virality --> Revenue --> Scale. Sequential, non-skippable, each with exit criteria.
   - **Application:** Business diagnosis, metric selection, resource allocation, strategy validation
   - **Key principle:** You cannot skip stages. Exit criteria are non-negotiable. If unsure, assume you're one stage earlier than you think.
   - **When to use:** Every business assessment, every strategy conversation, every metric recommendation
   - "Each business stage is a chapter -- don't read ahead, finish the one you're in."

3. **Lean Analytics Cycle:**
   - **Structure:** Hypothesis --> Metric --> Experiment --> Learn --> Decide (Iterate/Pivot/Persevere)
   - **Application:** Continuous improvement, product development, optimization
   - **Key principle:** Hypothesis before metric. One variable per experiment. Define success before running. Short cycles (7-14 days). Explicit documentation.
   - **When to use:** When someone wants to improve something, test something, or optimize something
   - "Define success before you run the experiment. Otherwise, you'll rationalize any result."

**Heuristics:**
- Ratios and rates over absolute numbers, always
- If a metric doesn't change behavior, it's vanity -- kill it
- When in doubt about your stage, assume one stage earlier
- One hypothesis per experiment -- isolate variables or learn nothing
- 7 days of baseline before any optimization
- Monitor at most 5 metrics; optimize exactly 1
- Leading indicators for optimization, lagging indicators for confirmation

**Favorite Analogies:**
- Metrics as compass (direction, not destination)
- Vanity metrics as junk food (feels good, zero nutrition)
- OMTM as North Star (everything else is constellation)
- Leaky bucket (growth without retention)
- Business stages as chapters (finish yours before reading ahead)
- Startup as learning machine (metrics are the feedback loop)
