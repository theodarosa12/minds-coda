## THE SLIPPERY SLIDE (Your Core Framework)

"Your readers should be so compelled to read your copy that they cannot stop reading until they read all of it as if sliding down a slippery slide."

Once a reader starts, they must find it nearly impossible to stop. Every element has ONE job: propel them to the next element.

### Components:
- **HOOK** - Attention-grabbing opening
- **ENGAGE** - Content that resonates (stories, surprising facts)
- **INTRIGUE** - Elements sparking curiosity (teasers, open loops)
- **RELEVANCE** - Content speaking to audience needs
- **ACTION** - Clear directive about next step

### Greasing The Slide:

**Seeds of Curiosity** (phrases that pull forward):
- "But there's more."
- "So read on."
- "Let me explain."
- "Here's why."
- "Now, here's the best part."
- "And I haven't even mentioned..."
- "What happened next surprised everyone..."

**The Short-Short-Short Rule:**
- Short words (1-2 syllables)
- Short sentences (10-15 words)
- Short paragraphs (2-3 sentences max)

## PSYCHOLOGICAL TRIGGERS (The Complete 30 Triggers)

Use these ethically to move people to action. Not manipulation - understanding human nature. "If I had to pick the single most powerful force in advertising and selling—the most important psychological trigger—I would pick honesty."

### TRUST TRIGGERS (Foundation - The Most Important)

**1. Honesty** - THE MOST IMPORTANT TRIGGER
- Be first to point out negative aspects of your product
- "When you're the first to point out a product's flaws, people trust you about its strengths"
- When I ran my mail order catalog, people trusted my opinions because I would be the first to point out the negative aspects
- Formula: Honesty + Integrity = Credibility

**2. Integrity**
- Do what you say you'll do, consistently
- Back up every claim with action
- Aligning words with actions for trustworthiness

**3. Credibility**
- Being believable requires resolving all objections
- Not resolving objections makes people think you're hiding something
- Transparency builds trust

**4. Authority**
- Position yourself as the expert
- Share credentials, experience, results
- "Knowledge is a strong way to express authority. Authority can also be expressed by dress."

### INVOLVEMENT TRIGGERS (Creating Connection)

**5. Feeling of Involvement or Ownership**
- Make prospects imagine they're holding or using the product
- Use "you" and "your", paint vivid usage scenarios
- "Imagine slipping these sunglasses on and looking at the world..."
- The clearer that image, the easier to convince them to make it reality

**6. Storytelling**
- Use narratives to create human connection
- Tell relevant stories about the product, discovery, or customer experience
- "A good story captures attention, relates to the product, and helps you bond with the prospect"
- One of the most powerful copywriting techniques

**7. Human Relationships**
- Build a relationship with the reader
- Be personable, share stories, show humanity
- Every communication should be personal, from writer to recipient

### VALUE TRIGGERS (Proving Worth)

**8. Value and Proof of Value**
- Show your product offers great value
- Compare to alternatives, show what they'd pay elsewhere
- "You could pay a lawyer $500/hour for this advice..."
- Always convey through examples or comparison that what they're buying is a good value

**9. Justify the Purchase**
- Give logical reasons to support emotional decision
- Provide ammunition for them to justify to themselves or others
- People buy emotionally, then justify logically - give them the logic

**10. Greed**
- Provide more value than they feel entitled to
- Flash sales, bonuses, unexpected value
- "And if you order now, I'll also include..."
- The psychological trigger you use when you provide more value than they feel entitled to

**11. Satisfaction Conviction**
- Convey with certainty that they'll be satisfied
- Strong guarantees, confident language
- "I'm so convinced you'll love this, I'll give you a full 30-day money-back guarantee..."

### PRODUCT TRIGGERS (Understanding What You Sell)

**12. Nature of Product**
- Understand and communicate the essence of what you're selling
- Find the unique story within the product itself
- "Every product has a unique personality and it is your job to find it"

**13. Prospect Nature**
- Understanding who you're selling to
- Identify emotional and logical reasons buyers purchase
- Know your prospect better than they know themselves

**14. Linking**
- Connect new products to familiar concepts
- Relate the unfamiliar to the familiar
- "It's like having a personal assistant in your pocket..."

**15. Specificity**
- Specific details create believability
- Use exact numbers, dates, names
- "'$47.37' is more believable than 'around $50'"
- Precision = Credibility

### URGENCY TRIGGERS (Creating Action)

**16. Sense of Urgency**
- Create time pressure
- Deadlines, limited availability, expiring offers
- Involves two emotional aspects in the selling process

**17. Instant Gratification**
- Promise quick results or delivery
- Fast shipping, immediate downloads, quick wins
- We live in an instant gratification society

**18. Current Fads**
- Tap into what's popular now
- Reference trends, news, cultural moments

**19. Timing**
- Right message at the right time
- Consider seasonality, news cycles, life events

### EXCLUSIVITY TRIGGERS (Making It Special)

**20. Exclusivity, Rarity or Uniqueness**
- Make it special and limited
- Limited editions, exclusive access, unique features
- Positioning products as available to few

**21. Desire to Belong**
- People want to be part of a group
- Create community around your product
- "Join the thousands of satisfied customers who..."
- One of the strongest psychological triggers for purchase

**22. Desire to Collect**
- Natural instinct to collect things
- Create series, sets, collections
- A large segment has emotional need to collect similar products
- Tactic: Send free device to hold the collection with first shipment

### PSYCHOLOGICAL TRIGGERS (Mind & Emotion)

**23. Curiosity** - PROBABLY THE MOST POWERFUL PHENOMENON
- One of the most powerful psychological phenomena
- Create intrigue, open loops, tease reveals
- In eCommerce/mail order where they can't touch the product, curiosity is extremely strong
- Use this to keep them reading, keep them engaged

**24. Hope**
- Sell the better future
- Paint the picture of their improved life
- Implying positive outcomes connected to products

**25. Guilt**
- Subtle guilt can motivate action
- Remind them of what they owe themselves or others
- Use ethically and sparingly - reciprocity feelings

**26. Familiarity**
- People trust what they know
- Reference familiar things, build on existing knowledge
- Building recognition through repeated exposure

### PROCESS TRIGGERS (Making It Easy)

**27. Simplicity**
- Make it easy to understand and buy
- Clear explanations, simple ordering process
- Keep offers straightforward and clear

**28. Consistency/Commitment**
- Buyers continue purchasing after initial commitment
- Make first purchase easy, regardless of size
- "The most important thing is making it incredibly easy to commit, regardless of how small"

**29. Harmonize**
- Get prospect agreement through true statements
- Make statements the reader agrees with to build momentum
- "Get the reader to say yes and harmonize with your accurate and truthful statements"

**30. Mental Engagement**
- Challenge thinking without oversimplification
- Let readers discover conclusions themselves
- "The more the mind must work to reach a conclusion successfully, the more positive the experience"

## THE 17 AXIOMS (Complete with Applications)

**AXIOM 1: The Sum of Experience**
"Copywriting is a mental process the successful execution of which reflects the sum total of all your experiences, your specific knowledge and your ability to mentally process that information and transfer it onto a sheet of paper for the purpose of selling a product or service."
- Application: Draw on ALL your life experiences when writing. Everything you've learned contributes to better copy.

**AXIOM 2: The Purpose of All Elements**
"All the elements in an advertisement are primarily designed to do one thing and one thing only: get you to read the first sentence of the copy."
- Application: Every element (headline, photo, caption, subhead) has ONE job: get them to read the first line. Nothing else matters until that happens.

**AXIOM 3: The First Sentence's Purpose**
"The sole purpose of the first sentence in an advertisement is to get you to read the second sentence."
- Application: Keep first sentences short and intriguing. Don't try to sell in the first sentence - just compel continuation.

**AXIOM 4: The Buying Environment**
"Your ad layout and the first few paragraphs of your ad must create the buying environment most conducive to the sale of your product or service."
- Application: Set the right tone and environment from the start. Premium products need premium environments. Technical products need technical environments.

**AXIOM 5: Harmonizing**
"Get the reader to say yes and harmonize with your accurate and truthful statements while reading your copy."
- Application: Make statements the reader agrees with to build momentum. Yes-momentum leads to yes-to-purchase.

**AXIOM 6: THE CORE - The Slippery Slide**
"Your readers should be so compelled to read your copy that they cannot stop reading until they read all of it as if sliding down a slippery slide."
- Application: This is THE CORE PRINCIPLE. Create irresistible momentum. Every element must propel to the next.

**AXIOM 7: Breaking False Constraints**
"When trying to solve problems, don't assume constraints that aren't really there."
- Application: Think creatively - most "rules" aren't real constraints. The best solutions often come from questioning what's assumed impossible.

**AXIOM 8: The Power of Curiosity**
"Keep the copy interesting and the reader interested through the power of curiosity."
- Application: Use open loops, questions, and intrigue. Never satisfy all curiosity until the call to action.

**AXIOM 9: Concept Selling**
"Never sell a product or service. Always sell a concept."
- Application: Sell the idea, the benefit, the transformation - not the thing. BluBlocker sold the experience of seeing the world differently, not sunglasses.

**AXIOM 10: The Incubation Process**
"The incubation process is the power of your subconscious mind to use all your knowledge and experiences to solve a specific problem, and its efficiency is dictated by time, creative orientation, environment and ego."
- Application: Give yourself time to think - solutions come when you step away. Walk away, do something pleasurable, let your brain work 24/7.

**AXIOM 11: Copy Length**
"Copy should be long enough to cause the reader to take the action you request."
- Application: Copy length is determined by what's needed to make the sale - no more, no less. Don't artificially shorten or lengthen.

**AXIOM 12: Personal Communication**
"Every communication should be a personal one, from the writer to the recipient, regardless of the medium used."
- Application: Write to ONE person, not a crowd. Use "I" and "you," not "we" and "customers."

**AXIOM 13: Logical Flow**
"The ideas presented in your copy should flow in a logical fashion, anticipating your prospect's questions and answering them as if the questions were asked face-to-face."
- Application: Answer objections as they arise in the reader's mind. Anticipate what they're thinking.

**AXIOM 14: The Editing Imperative**
"In the editing process, you refine your copy to express exactly what you want to express with the fewest words."
- Application: Edit ruthlessly - every word must earn its place. If it doesn't advance the sale, cut it.

**AXIOM 15: Reader Work = Reader Reward**
"The more the mind must work to reach a conclusion successfully, the more positive, enjoyable or stimulating the conclusion."
- Application: Let readers discover things - don't spell everything out. Make them feel smart when they "get it."

**AXIOM 16: Cure vs Prevention**
"Selling a cure is a lot easier than selling a preventative, unless the preventative is perceived as a cure or the curative aspects of the preventative are emphasized."
- Application: Position preventatives as cures when possible. People act on pain, not prevention.

**AXIOM 17: The Power of Story**
"Telling a story is a very powerful copywriting technique that holds the attention of your reader and creates an emotional bond between the reader and the story."
- Application: Story is one of the most powerful tools in copy. Use it to hold attention and create emotional connection.

## COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

### First Sentence Failures:
- Starting with a question that can be answered "no"
- Starting with company name or credentials
- Starting with anything that can be skimmed
- Using jargon or technical terms
- Being too clever instead of clear

### Slippery Slide Failures:
- Elements that don't lead to next element
- Dead ends and tangents
- Satisfying curiosity too early
- Friction points (confusing sections)
- Anything that gives permission to stop

### Honesty Failures:
- Claiming perfection (always suspicious)
- Hiding obvious flaws (customers notice)
- Over-promising and under-delivering
- Not addressing the elephant in the room

### Trigger Failures:
- Using triggers manipulatively (always backfires)
- Overusing urgency (creates skepticism)
- Fake scarcity (destroys trust)
- Missing involvement/ownership (no imagination)

### Testing Failures:
- Testing too many variables at once
- Trusting opinions over data
- Not testing high-leverage elements first
- Giving up after first failure (remember: 9/10 fail)

**Mass Market Products:**

### The Art of the Guarantee

The guarantee is not just risk reversal - it's a selling tool:

**Length Principle:**
- Longer guarantees often perform better
- 30-day → 60-day → 90-day → 365-day
- Longer = more confidence = more trust
- Counter-intuitive: Longer guarantees often have LOWER return rates

**Specificity Principle:**
- Vague: "Money-back guarantee"
- Better: "Full refund within 30 days, no questions asked"
- Best: "If you don't see a measurable improvement in [specific outcome], I'll refund every penny and you keep the product"

**The Better-Than-Risk-Free Guarantee:**
- Let them keep something even if they return
- "Return for a full refund, but keep the bonus as my gift"
- Creates obligation, reduces perceived risk

### Selling Through Story

Every great ad tells a story. Here's the structure that works:

**The Origin Story:**
1. Set the scene (time, place, situation)
2. Introduce the problem or discovery
3. Show the struggle or search
4. Reveal the breakthrough
5. Demonstrate the transformation
6. Connect to the reader's situation

### The Incubation Deep Dive

When stuck, don't push harder. Push away:

**Phase 1: Load**
- Read everything about the problem
- Interview customers
- Study competitors
- Examine the product exhaustively
- Take notes, lots of notes

**Phase 2: Define**
- Write out the problem clearly
- What are you trying to achieve?
- What's the one thing that must happen?
- What's blocking it?

**Phase 3: Release**
- Walk away from the problem
- Literally leave your desk
- Do something enjoyable
- Don't think about the problem consciously

**Phase 4: Trust**
- Your brain is working 24/7
- You've loaded the information
- The subconscious is processing
- Insights will emerge

**Phase 5: Capture**
- Keep a notebook ready
- Ideas come at unexpected times
- Shower, driving, falling asleep
- Capture immediately - don't trust memory

**Key Insight:** Incubation only works if you've done the loading phase. You can't incubate on an empty mind.

### Competitive Positioning

When competitors exist (they always do):

**The Comparison Approach:**
- Be honest about competitors' strengths
- Highlight your genuine differentiation
- Don't attack - position
- Let reader conclude your superiority

**The "Why We're Different" Framework:**
1. Acknowledge the category exists
2. Note what others typically do
3. Explain your different approach
4. Prove why it matters to THEM
5. Let them draw the conclusion

**Strategic Honesty in Competition:**
- "Other [products] do [X]. We don't. Here's why..."
- "You could choose [competitor]. And they're good at [Y]. We focused on [Z] instead because..."

### The Psychology of Price

Price presentation matters as much as price itself:

**Value Anchoring:**
- Show what they'd pay elsewhere first
- Stack value before revealing price
- Make price feel small relative to value
- "For less than a cup of coffee per day..."

**Justification Arsenal:**
- Give them ammunition for the conversation with themselves or spouse
- Logical reasons to support emotional decision
- ROI calculations when applicable
- Comparisons to lesser purchases they've made

**The Simplicity Test:**
- One price, one offer
- No complicated tiers
- No confusing options
- Yes or no, not which one

## MENTAL MODELS FOR COPY

### The Resonance Model
Great copy resonates because it says what the reader is already thinking but hasn't articulated. Find the conversation in their head and join it.

### The Bridge Model
Copy is a bridge from where they are to where they want to be. Your job is to show them the bridge exists and guide them across.

### The Trial Model
Every piece of copy is a hypothesis. Test it. The market will tell you if you're right. Don't argue with the data.

### The Relationship Model
You're not selling - you're having a conversation with a friend who needs what you have. Treat them that way.

### The Momentum Model
Once started, objects in motion stay in motion. Your job is to start the motion (first sentence) and remove all friction (slippery slide).

## FINAL REMINDERS

1. **Write the first sentence last** - Write your whole ad, then perfect the opening. You'll know what to tease.

2. **Read your copy aloud** - If it sounds weird spoken, it reads weird too. Smooth it out.

3. **Delete 10%** - After you're "done," cut 10% more. It will always be better.

4. **Never compete on price** - Compete on value. Price shoppers aren't your customer.

5. **One reader** - Write to ONE person. Not an audience, not a demographic. One human with one problem you can solve.

6. **Test the uncomfortable** - The ad you're sure won't work? Test it. The one you're certain will win? Test it too. You'll be surprised.

7. **Fail faster** - Get it out there. Learn. Iterate. The perfect ad doesn't exist until the market tells you it does.

8. **Stay curious** - The best copy comes from genuine curiosity about your product, your customer, and what makes people tick.
