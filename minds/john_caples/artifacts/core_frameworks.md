# Core Frameworks - John Caples

## 1. The 4 Functions of Headlines

Every headline must perform these four functions simultaneously:

### Function 1: Get ATTENTION
- Stop the scanner
- Interrupt the browsing pattern
- Make the reader PAUSE
- Techniques: specific numbers, power words, curiosity gaps, urgency
- Test: "If someone were flipping pages quickly, would this headline make them STOP?"

### Function 2: SELECT the Right Audience
- Flag exactly the people you want to reach
- Repel those who are not prospects
- Speak directly to the ideal buyer's situation
- Techniques: mention the audience, reference their specific problem, use niche language
- Test: "Will this headline attract the RIGHT prospect and repel the WRONG one?"

### Function 3: Deliver a COMPLETE Message
- Many people read ONLY the headline
- The headline must work as a standalone advertisement
- It should communicate the core promise completely
- Techniques: full benefit in headline, clear promise, self-interest evident
- Test: "If someone read ONLY this headline, would they understand the offer?"

### Function 4: DRAW the Reader Into the Body Copy
- Create irresistible curiosity to continue reading
- Open a loop that the body copy closes
- Promise more value inside
- Techniques: incomplete curiosity, partial promise, unfinished story
- Test: "Does this headline create an IRRESISTIBLE urge to read more?"

### Scoring
- Score each headline 0-10 on each function
- Total possible: 40/40
- Below 25: Rewrite immediately
- 25-32: Acceptable with optimization
- 33-40: Strong headline, ready to test

## 2. The 8 Proven Headline Formulas

### Formula 1: How-To
**Template:** "How to [achieve desired result]"
**Why it works:** Direct educational promise. Universal appeal.
**Examples:**
- "How to Win Friends and Influence People"
- "How to Stop Worrying and Start Living"
- "How to Improve Your Memory in One Evening"
**Best for:** Products that teach, educate, or enable

### Formula 2: They Laughed When (Story Transformation)
**Template:** "They Laughed When I [did unexpected thing] But When I [achieved result]!"
**Why it works:** Combines story, social proof, transformation, and revenge in one headline
**Examples:**
- "They Laughed When I Sat Down At the Piano But When I Started to Play!"
- "They All Laughed When I Spoke French — But Their Laughter Turned to Amazement"
**Best for:** Personal transformation, skill development, overcoming doubt

### Formula 3: News Announcement
**Template:** "Announcing..." / "New..." / "At Last..." / "Now..." / "Finally..."
**Why it works:** Novelty triggers automatic attention response
**Examples:**
- "Announcing a New Way to Learn Piano — In 30 Days"
- "At Last — A Way to Earn Money While You Sleep"
- "New Discovery Makes Growing Hair Easy"
**Best for:** Product launches, improvements, genuine innovations

### Formula 4: Question
**Template:** "Do You [make common mistake/have common problem]?"
**Why it works:** Engages reader directly, activates curiosity and self-evaluation
**Examples:**
- "Do You Make These Mistakes in English?"
- "Can You Pass This Simple Test of English?"
- "Are You Ever Tongue-Tied at a Party?"
**Best for:** Problem identification, creating self-awareness, engaging emotions

### Formula 5: Command
**Template:** "[Strong verb] + [desired result]!"
**Why it works:** Authority + urgency + clear direction
**Examples:**
- "Stop Worrying and Start Living!"
- "Throw Away Your Oars!"
- "Give Me 5 Days and I'll Give You a Magnetic Personality"
**Best for:** When prospects need direct push to action, overcoming inertia

### Formula 6: Reason Why
**Template:** "[Number] Reasons Why [claim/promise]"
**Why it works:** Specific numbers + logic + promise of organized value
**Examples:**
- "7 Reasons Why This Investment Doubles Your Money"
- "161 New Ways to a Man's Heart — in This Fascinating Book for Cooks"
- "6 Types of Investors — Which Group Are You In?"
**Best for:** Building logical case, multiple benefits, sophisticated buyers

### Formula 7: Testimonial
**Template:** "'[First person result statement]' - [Attribution]"
**Why it works:** Third-party proof, credibility, identification
**Examples:**
- "'I Lost 20 Pounds in 30 Days Using This Simple Method'"
- "'I Make $2,000 a Month at Home'"
- "'I Couldn't Believe My Eyes When I Read My First Commission Check'"
**Best for:** When you have powerful real results to showcase

### Formula 8: Offer/Free
**Template:** "Free [valuable thing] Shows You How to [desired result]"
**Why it works:** Immediate value + low risk + concrete promise
**Examples:**
- "Free Book Shows You 12 Secrets of Better Cooking"
- "Free: A New System for Learning Languages at Home"
- "Free Report Reveals 7 Tax Loopholes Your Accountant May Not Know"
**Best for:** Lead generation, top of funnel, first contact, list building

## 3. Split-Run Testing Methodology

### The Fundamental Principle
"Test, don't guess. The difference between the right appeal and the wrong appeal can be 19.5x in results."

### Test Design Protocol

#### Step 1: Isolate ONE Variable
- Change ONLY one element per test
- If testing headlines, keep everything else identical
- If testing illustrations, keep headline and copy identical
- If testing offers, keep headline and body identical

#### Step 2: Establish Control
- Current best-performing version = Control
- All new tests compete against the control
- Winner becomes new control
- Process never stops

#### Step 3: Ensure Statistical Significance
- Minimum sample size before drawing conclusions
- Run tests long enough to account for day-of-week variation
- Don't declare winner after 10 responses
- In digital: use standard statistical calculators

#### Step 4: Measure the RIGHT Metric
- Mail order: Count coupons returned
- Digital ads: Count conversions, not clicks
- Email: Count sales, not opens
- The only metric that matters is the one closest to money

#### Step 5: Document and Iterate
- Record ALL test results (winners AND losers)
- Build institutional knowledge of what works
- Each winning element becomes new control to beat
- Testing never stops — markets change, audiences evolve

### What to Test (Priority Order)
1. **Headlines** — 50-75% of ad success (test FIRST)
2. **Offers** — What you're offering changes everything
3. **Illustrations** — Visual impact matters
4. **Copy length** — Long vs short for YOUR product
5. **Layout** — Arrangement affects reading flow
6. **Price presentation** — How price is framed

## 4. Self-Interest Appeal Framework

### The Core Principle
"The reader wants to know: What's In It For Me?"

### Self-Interest Hierarchy (Tested)
1. **Making money** — "$31,250 the first year"
2. **Saving money** — "Cut your bills in half"
3. **Health/appearance** — "Look 10 years younger"
4. **Saving time** — "Learn in 15 minutes"
5. **Reducing effort** — "While you sleep"
6. **Gaining pleasure** — "Enjoy restaurant meals at home"
7. **Avoiding pain** — "Stop embarrassing mistakes"
8. **Being popular** — "Win friends instantly"
9. **Being admired** — "They couldn't believe it was me"
10. **Having security** — "Never worry about money again"

### How to Apply
1. Identify the #1 self-interest appeal for your product
2. Put it in the headline — not buried in body copy
3. Be SPECIFIC about the benefit ("$31,250" not "good income")
4. Frame it as reader benefit, not product feature
5. Test different appeals against each other

## 5. Before-and-After Structure

### Template
1. **BEFORE:** Paint the pain/problem vividly (reader identifies)
2. **BRIDGE:** Introduce the solution/product
3. **AFTER:** Paint the result/transformation vividly
4. **PROOF:** Testimonials, data, specifics that prove the after
5. **CTA:** Tell them exactly how to get from Before to After

### Why It Works
- Activates empathy (BEFORE = "that's me")
- Creates desire for transformation
- Makes the product the logical bridge
- The AFTER sells the product, not the features

### Example Structure
"Are you tired of [BEFORE problem]? Imagine if you could [AFTER result]. 
Now you can, with [BRIDGE product]. [PROOF: specific numbers, testimonials].
[CTA: Here's how to start]."

## 6. The Subheads-as-Second-Headlines System

### Principle
"Many readers scan subheads before deciding whether to read the full copy. Each subhead must work as an independent headline."

### Rules for Subheads
1. Place every 3-4 paragraphs in long copy
2. Each subhead must be interesting ALONE
3. Subheads should tell a complete story when read sequentially
4. Use self-interest appeals in subheads
5. Include specific numbers when possible
6. A reader who reads ONLY the headline and subheads should understand the full offer

### Test
Read only your headline and subheads aloud. Do they tell a compelling, complete story? If not, rewrite the subheads.

## 7. First Paragraph Continuation Principle

### The Rule
"The first paragraph of body copy MUST continue the promise made in the headline. Do not change subject. Do not introduce new topics. AMPLIFY what the headline started."

### Why It Works
- The headline got them interested in a specific promise
- Changing direction = breaking the promise = losing the reader
- Continuation builds momentum
- Reader's attention is fragile — reward it immediately

### Example
**Headline:** "How to Lose 12 Pounds in 60 Days Without Dieting"
**First paragraph:** "A simple method discovered by researchers at [university] allows you to lose weight naturally without giving up any of your favorite foods. In a study of 847 participants..."

NOT: "Our company was founded in 1985 with a vision to..." (FAILS — breaks headline promise)

## 8. Specific Numbers Framework

### The Principle
"Specific numbers create instant credibility. Vague claims create instant skepticism."

### Conversion Table
| Vague (Weak) | Specific (Strong) |
|---|---|
| "a lot of money" | "$31,250" |
| "many people" | "12,847 people" |
| "quickly" | "in 15 minutes" |
| "much better" | "19.5 times better" |
| "lose weight" | "lose 12 pounds" |
| "save time" | "save 3 hours a week" |
| "experts agree" | "47 scientists confirmed" |
| "most customers" | "89% of customers" |

### Why It Works
- Specificity implies measurement
- Measurement implies truth
- Truth creates credibility
- Credibility creates trust
- Trust creates sales

### Application
1. In headlines: "How to Earn $50,000 From Home" > "How to Earn Good Money From Home"
2. In claims: "47 studies prove..." > "Studies prove..."
3. In testimonials: "'I lost 23 pounds in 45 days'" > "'I lost a lot of weight'"
4. In offers: "Save $14.95" > "Save money"
5. In guarantees: "60-day money-back guarantee" > "Satisfaction guaranteed"
